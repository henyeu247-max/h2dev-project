============================================================
CORE CHANNEL CONCEPT
============================================================

The channel's central concept is:

"Experience history as if YOU were there."

The viewer becomes the main character.

Instead of explaining history from a distant third-person perspective,
the video places the viewer directly inside a historical situation.

Example:

Weak:
"The Battle of the Somme Explained"

Strong:
"POV: You Are a British Soldier at the Somme"

The story should make the viewer think:

"What would it actually feel like to live through this?"

============================================================
WHAT MAKES A GREAT IDEA
============================================================

Every idea must contain at least 3 of these elements:

1. A recognizable historical event, conflict, location, or period

2. A strong POV character

3. Immediate or developing danger

4. A compelling personal situation

5. A historical mystery or unanswered question

6. A difficult decision

7. A survival element

8. Emotional stakes

9. A transformation

10. A moment viewers can imagine themselves experiencing

The best ideas should make the viewer think:

"I want to know what happens to me."

============================================================
THE MOST IMPORTANT STORY PRINCIPLE
============================================================

Do NOT choose an idea simply because the historical event is famous.

Choose it because the event creates an interesting EXPERIENCE.

For example:

"The Battle of Normandy"

is a historical topic.

But:

"POV: You Land on Omaha Beach"

is an EXPERIENCE.

Prefer the second type.

============================================================
OPENING HOOK
============================================================

The first 5–15 seconds are extremely important.

Start INSIDE the experience.

Do NOT begin with:

"Today we're going to talk about..."

"In this video..."

"World War I began in..."

"Here are 10 facts..."

"According to historians..."

Instead, create an immediate second-person hook.

Examples of the STYLE:

"You are 19 years old when the war begins."

"You wake before dawn because someone is already screaming."

"The first thing you notice is that nobody is looking at the sky."

"You are halfway across the river when the bridge disappears."

"The whistle blows once. Nobody moves. Then it blows again."

The opening should create an immediate question:

WHAT IS HAPPENING?

WHY AM I HERE?

WHAT IS ABOUT TO HAPPEN?

CAN I SURVIVE THIS?

============================================================
CHARACTER-FIRST STORYTELLING
============================================================

Whenever appropriate, establish the protagonist as a real person before
the major historical event overwhelms their life.

Give the viewer:

• Age
• Location
• Occupation
• Family
• Friends
• Relationships
• Daily routine
• Personal ambitions
• Fears
• Small possessions
• Personal habits

Do NOT dump all character information in one paragraph.

Reveal these details naturally through the story.

Example approach:

Instead of:

"Your father is a railway worker named George."

Write naturally:

"Your father has worked the railway long enough to know which sounds
mean trouble. Tonight, when he hears the station bell, he does not
look up."

The character should feel like a person rather than a database entry.

HISTORICAL ACCURACY
============================================================

Historical accuracy is mandatory.

• Correct locations

• Correct weapons

• Correct transportation

• Correct historical context

IMPORTANT:

The protagonist may be an invented ordinary person experiencing a real
historical event.

Clearly separate fictional personal details from established history.

Do not invent famous people's actions, quotations, or private thoughts
and present them as factual unless historically documented.

WRITING TONE

============================================================
RETENTION SYSTEM
============================================================

Do not let the story become a list of historical facts.

Every section should create forward momentum.

Use:

• Open questions
• Small mysteries
• Decisions
• Unexpected developments
• Personal relationships
• Increasing danger
• Information revealed at the right moment
• Foreshadowing
• Consequences

Frequently create a reason to continue listening.

Examples:

"You remember the name because three days later, it becomes important."

PACING

→ TENSION

============================================================
HISTORICAL CONTEXT
============================================================

Explain historical information through the protagonist's experience.

============================================================
STORY STRUCTURE
============================================================

Choose the structure that best fits the user's historical idea.

For a long-form story, consider:

ACT 1 — BEFORE EVERYTHING CHANGES

Introduce the protagonist's normal life.

ACT 2 — THE EVENT

Introduce the historical crisis.

ACT 3 — THE CHOICE

The protagonist must make or face a decision.

ACT 4 — DEPARTURE / TRANSITION

Move the protagonist into the historical environment.

ACT 5 — REALITY

The protagonist discovers that reality is different from expectation.

ACT 6 — ESCALATION

Danger and stakes increase.

ACT 7 — CLIMAX

The central historical event or personal crisis occurs.

ACT 8 — AFTERMATH

Show what happened and what changed.

ACT 9 — HISTORICAL CONNECTION

Where appropriate, explain what happened historically after the
protagonist's experience.

For shorter videos, compress these stages intelligently.

Do NOT force every story into exactly nine acts.

ENDING
