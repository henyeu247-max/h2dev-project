# PILOT 01: The Dark and Secret History of the Mirror
## Niche: Everyday History & Origin Stories
## Target Duration: 10 - 12 Minutes (~1,400 - 1,600 words)
## Format: Voiceover + Visual Segments (separated by ===)

=== SEGMENT 01: The Reflection Paradox (00:00 - 01:15)
For thousands of years of human existence, no one on Earth knew what they actually looked like. If you wanted to see your own face, your only option was a calm puddle of dark water, or a polished piece of black volcanic obsidian. And even then, what looked back at you was murky, distorted, and fleeting.
=== SEGMENT 02: When Mirrors Were Worth More Than Mansions (01:15 - 02:45)
In the 16th century, a single Venetian mirror was worth more than an aristocratic estate or a warship. The island of Murano in Venice held a brutal, state-enforced monopoly. Glassmakers were forbidden from leaving under penalty of death. If an artisan escaped, assassins were dispatched across Europe to slit their throat.
=== SEGMENT 03: The Toxic Formula and Poisoned Artisans (02:45 - 04:30)
The secret to true reflection was toxic. Artisans used an amalgam of tin and liquid mercury. Workers inhaled deadly mercury fumes day and night, suffering tremors, tooth loss, and madness. Most glassmakers died before reaching the age of 30. The luxury of seeing yourself clearly was literally paved with blood and poison.
=== SEGMENT 04: The French Espionage and The Hall of Mirrors (04:30 - 06:15)
King Louis XIV of France could not bear to send French gold to Venice. His chief minister Colbert launched a covert espionage operation, bribing Venetian masters to defect to Paris. Venice retaliated with poison, but the secret was out. The result was the Hall of Mirrors at Versailles—a colossal propaganda statement that France had conquered light itself.
=== SEGMENT 05: The Vampire Myth and The Silver Revolution (06:15 - 08:00)
In 1835, German chemist Justus von Liebig invented the modern silvering process using a thin chemical layer of real silver. Because folklore believed silver was a pure metal that rejected the unholy, the legend was born that vampires had no reflection in silver mirrors. Overnight, mirrors shifted from an elite royal weapon to a cheap household commodity.
=== SEGMENT 06: The Psychology of The Double (08:00 - 09:45)
When mirrors entered everyday homes, human psychology permanently changed. For the first time, people developed a third-person awareness of their own aging, self-criticism, and personal vanity. The mirror ceased to be an object—it became an internal judge.
=== SEGMENT 07: Conclusion & The Endless Gaze (09:45 - 11:00)
Today, you glance at a mirror dozens of times a day without thinking. But behind that smooth pane of glass lies 500 years of assassination, chemical madness, and imperial espionage. Next time you look at your reflection, remember: seeing yourself was once humanity's deadliest obsession.
